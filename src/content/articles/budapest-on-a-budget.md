Budapest is one of the best-value capitals in the EU. You get grand architecture, thermal baths and a lively food scene for noticeably less than Vienna or Prague. It's also easy to overspend through bad exchange rates, tourist-trap restaurants and taxis. Here's what a trip really costs and how to keep it affordable.

The biggest variables are **where you sleep** and **whether you eat on the main tourist streets**.

## Accommodation

- **Hostels** (dorm beds) are the cheapest option and often very good quality in Budapest.
- **Apartments** are great value for two or more people, especially in Districts VI and VII.
- **Budget hotels** are good if you want a reception desk and daily cleaning.

**Where to stay:**
- **District VII (Erzsébetváros):** the ruin-bar district. Cheap and central, but **noisy at night**. Ask for a room facing the courtyard.
- **District VI (Terézváros):** around Andrássy Avenue. Central, a little calmer, great transport.
- **District VIII (Palace District, near Múzeum körút):** quieter and often cheaper, still walkable to the centre.
- **District V:** next to the Danube and Parliament. Beautiful, but you pay for the location.

## Food and drink

- **Lunch menus:** many restaurants offer a weekday *napi menü* (soup plus main) at a much lower price than ordering à la carte.
- **Street food:** try *lángos* (fried dough, often topped with sour cream and cheese) and *kürtőskalács* (chimney cake).
- **Check the bill:** many restaurants add a service charge (*szervízdíj*) automatically. If it's already on the bill, you don't need to tip again.
- **Avoid** restaurants with menus in five languages and staff inviting you in on Váci utca. Walk two streets away.
- **Halal options:** there are Turkish, Middle Eastern and South Asian restaurants, especially around District VII and VIII. Look for halal certification or ask.

## Transport

- **From the airport:** the **100E** express bus goes straight to Deák Ferenc tér in the centre. It needs its own special ticket; regular tickets and passes aren't valid on it.
- **In the city:** a 24-hour or 72-hour travel card covers the metro, trams and buses, and pays for itself quickly.
- **Tram 2** runs along the Pest side of the Danube, with views of Parliament and the castle. It's sightseeing for the price of a ticket.
- **Taxis:** use an app (such as Bolt) or call an official company. Don't take random taxis waiting at tourist spots.

**The baths:** pick one, not three.
- **Széchenyi:** the famous one, with big outdoor pools. It's the busiest and the most expensive, and weekend prices are higher.
- **Rudas:** smaller, with a historic Ottoman-era pool and a rooftop pool with Danube views.
- **Lukács:** more local, with fewer tourists.

Go on a **weekday morning** for lower prices and smaller crowds. Bring your own towel and flip-flops to avoid rental fees.

**Free things to do:**
- Walk the **Buda Castle district** streets and terraces.
- The **lower terraces of Fisherman's Bastion** are free; only the upper turrets are paid.
- Climb **Gellért Hill** to the Liberty Statue for the best sunset view of the city.
- See the **Shoes on the Danube Bank** memorial and Parliament from the outside.
- Spend an afternoon on **Margaret Island**, a car-free park in the middle of the Danube.
- Join a **tip-based free walking tour** to get your bearings on day one.

## Ways to save

1. **Pay in forints (HUF).** When a card machine or ATM asks, choose HUF, never euros. Avoid stand-alone ATMs with no bank logo (often bright yellow or blue) in tourist areas.
2. **Don't change money at the airport or on Váci utca.** Pay by card instead.
3. **Make lunch your main meal** and use the daily lunch menus.
4. **Go to the baths on a weekday.**
5. **Buy a 72-hour transport pass** instead of single tickets if you'll ride more than a few times a day.
6. **Supermarkets** (Spar, Lidl, Aldi) are cheap for water, snacks and breakfast.
